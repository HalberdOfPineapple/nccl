How to read the model

  • Copy throughput depends only on the source timers. It starts at the first CTA's startNs and ends at the last CTA's endNs. The destination's timestamps are never part of it.
  • The end time includes a round trip of signals. The last CTA's completion must reach the destination, and the acknowledgement must come back. Sending the reply takes the destination 1.57 µs here; its travel time is not measured separately.
  • All CTAs end together. One acknowledgement releases all 16, so they end within 0.13 µs in this transfer. A CTA that finishes issuing early simply waits longer.
  • A median can hide a group of CTAs. In this transfer 4 of the 16 CTAs finish issuing about 1400 µs early and then wait about 1430 µs. The same 4 SMs (44, 45, 88, 89) do this in all 12 transfers of all 3 runs at this setting, but not in the shared or crowded layouts at the same writer target. The cause was not studied.
  • issueEndNs on the destination has its own meaning. The field holds the time the receiver saw all 16 completions. Nothing is issued there.
  • The timestamps do not show when a store arrives. Stores get no reply, so no timer marks their arrival. The destination data check confirms afterwards that all values arrived correctly.
